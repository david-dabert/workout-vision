# WorkoutVision, the current state map (21 October 2026)

Asked by David on 21 October 2026: score every aspect of the app, go through the whole architecture, and say whether it is worth anything and whether it can become a commercial product.
Written on main at 779ebaa, after the merge of #241.

## How to read the scores

The scale, as David set it and as this map reads it:
- 1: what any competent team would ship, nothing distinctive.
- 4: works, unremarkable.
- 7: good, better than most products on the market.
- 10: the best in the world, the reference R16 names.

Method: seven readers, one per subsystem, read-only, each returning scores with evidence (file and line, command output, or URL); then three adversarial reviewers (a sports scientist, a product engineer who has shipped fitness apps, an investor doing due diligence) who re-opened every cited file, tried to refute each piece of evidence and gave their own score; then one completeness critic naming what no reader scored, the contradictions between readers, and the claims still unverified.
Two scores stand for every dimension: the reader's and the reviewer's. The reviewer's is the one to quote.
Counting numbers come only from the scoreboard files, with their date (R13): test/real-phone/accuracy/scoreboard.txt (19 October), public-scoreboard.txt (20 October), test/real-phone/synth/synth.txt (6 October).
Every published or commercial figure quoted from outside the repository is second-hand: no reader or reviewer could open an external page from the review container. They are listed under "Unverified" and must not be repeated as fact.

## The verdict in one page

What the app is today: the common open-source design, a per-exercise joint-angle hysteresis counter on Google's MediaPipe pose model, run on the phone, wrapped in a careful interface, native French, and an evidence system of unusual discipline; around it, nine demonstration modules that measure nothing from a real video, and a physiotherapy path that contradicts the product's own privacy promise.

Highest reviewed scores: 6, for the test and gate design and for accessibility. No dimension reaches 7. Reviewed mean over the 39 dimensions the readers scored: 3.1. Over the 14 dimensions the critic added: 2.1.

What has value, every reviewer agreeing:
1. The evidence apparatus: 1,064 public landmark sets in two fixed halves plus a never-read held-out half, 96 synthetic sets with exact truth, baselines read from the base branch so a change cannot lower its own bar, paired statistics printed beside every head, a register of 81 closed experiments. A team that already owns a counter and needs to qualify an engine would pay for this. It is public and MIT, so it is a credibility artefact, not a moat.
2. The honest interface: refusals, a confirm state with no grade, a sensitivity flag, the number withheld when the app cannot measure it. Defensible in a market where no commercial camera counter publishes an accuracy figure.
3. A pure, typed, on-device counting core under 6,000 lines that an auditor can read in a day, and native French on the live path.

What has no sellable value today:
1. The count: 12 of 15 of David's stored sets exact and 8 of 16 of his real videos (19 October), both in-sample against constants chosen while those sets were in view; 514 of 1,064 public sets (48 %, 20 October); shown as sure on 4 of 16 of his videos; right 61 % of the time it shows a public count as sure; a minimal detectable change of 2.4 reps across encodings, coarser than the one-rep progression the product exists to track.
2. Every measure beyond the count: range of motion 30 % low on synthetic truth, tempo 45 to 54 % short, no angle ever compared with a goniometer or motion capture; the numbers are nevertheless shown and exported.
3. The physiotherapy path: zero patient trials, 0 validated tolerances, a medical-device question (MDR class IIa) recorded on 1 October and unanswered, and a patient video upload that breaks the privacy promise (defect 1 below).
4. The data: one labeller, one body, zero exam sets of the 155 planned, zero blind sets; everything else is derived from public datasets and committed to a public repository.
5. The business: no entity, no trademark search, no payment path, no store listing, no measured users (about 100 by David's word, counts unverifiable), 2 stars and 0 forks.

The commercial answer: not a product and not an investable technology today. It is a credible foundation for one, in one of two shapes: a B2B counting core for a partner who controls the filming context (a coaching platform, a physiotherapy-software vendor, an equipment maker), qualified by the apparatus; or the consumer counter it was built as, once the count is proven out of sample. The physiotherapy market is the only place with willingness to pay in evidence, and it is also where the product violates its own written line.

The single thing that would most change the answer, named independently by all three reviewers: an out-of-sample exam on real phone video. At least 100 sets, at least two bodies per lift, filmed as the app instructs, counted by someone other than David before the app shows its number, run once. On it the app must be right whenever it says it is sure, and sure on most sets. PLAN.md has demanded this exam since September; no exam set exists.

## Defects found by this review that need action now (R16: no minor error left standing)

1. **Patient video uploaded with public access.** src/lib/kine/glimpse.js:43 uploads the patient's attempt video to a Vercel Blob store with `access: 'public'` before the proxy forwards it to a third-party model (Elorian); the sweep deletes it only within 30 minutes (glimpse-proxy/api/sweep.js). This breaks DIRECTIVES.md promise 6 and PLAN.md:12, is health data under GDPR, and sits outside HDS hosting. Whether the production build sets VITE_GLIMPSE_URL could not be read (the variables API refuses). David's decision: fix the access to private and record the vendor, or switch the feature off in production.
2. **Governance bypassed, 11 to 13 October.** 54 commits reached main without a pull request, 7 of them changing src/lib/counting and 4 realigning the scoreboard baselines in the same commit (R2, R4, R6, R7). They are by the account's second author, Azélie Bernard, who appears in no governance document (DIRECTIVES.md names David and Claude; AGENTS.md names Claude and Antigravity). Nothing prevents a repeat: main has four required checks and no required reviewer. Proposed: a required reviewer or a CODEOWNERS rule on src/lib/counting, and the second author named in the rules.
3. **The plan of record is stale.** PLAN.md on counter-core, the file every rule says prevails, was last edited on 30 September; the branch is 654 commits behind main and its head is by the second author. STATE.md, the file R11 says to re-read after every compaction, is dated 30 September (live commit 84e020a against 779ebaa). The current phase, which R7 and R12 depend on, is named nowhere authoritatively.
4. **R13 drift.** README.md:36 cites scoreboard.txt as 18 October while the file reads 19 October; README.md:54 says analytics are pinned off while deploy.yml:80 reads VITE_EVENTS_URL from a repository variable, so whether analytics run in production is stated two ways and known by nobody in the repository.
5. **No legal pages.** No mentions légales, no privacy policy, no terms, no identified publisher, no medical disclaimer on the fitness tests, the NIOSH limit, the calorie targets or the coaching contract template; the only "not a diagnosis" line is in the coaches' office copy.
6. **Licence notice missing.** public/web-demuxer.wasm contains FFmpeg-derived LGPL code (node_modules/web-demuxer/README.md:321); the repository carries no notice.
7. **The holdout discipline failed once.** test/real-phone/public/exposed.json records the whole CF-Rep holdout exposed on 4 October before the split.
8. **Raw ground truth has no custody.** David's labelled videos exist only on his phone and Mac; the repository holds landmarks only (.gitignore excludes all video). A lost device destroys provenance and any re-read with a better engine.
