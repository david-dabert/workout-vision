This document provides empirical market data and a competitive analysis for sports computer vision, athletic coaching software, and musculoskeletal rehabilitation technologies. ## 1. Personal Trainers & Éducateurs Sportifs in France

### 1.1 Statutory Framework & Population Size
In France, physical activity coaching for remuneration is strictly regulated under Article L. 212-1 of the *Code du Sport*. Practitioners must hold a state-recognized credential (BPJEPS, DEJEPS, CQP, or university STAPS degree) and maintain an active *Carte Professionnelle d'Éducateur Sportif* issued by the Ministry of Sports via the EAPS portal (*Établissements et Activités Physiques et Sportives*).

- **Total Active Professional Cards:** Between 110,000 and 125,000 active registered cards (with mandatory 5-year renewal cycles and automated casier judiciaire bulletin n°2 vetting).
- **Annual Inflow:** ~25,000 new declarations and renewals processed annually.
- **Employment Status Distribution:**
  - *Salaried (Associations, Municipalities, Clubs):* ~48%.
  - *Independent / Freelance (Micro-entreprises, EI, EURL):* ~52% (increased from 34% in 2017 to over 50% following post-2020 self-employment trends).
  - *Commercial Fitness Segment:* Approximately 22,000 personal trainers and group instructors operate within private commercial fitness centers.

### 1.2 Unit Economics for French Independent Coaches
Data compiled from INSEE (PCS 2020 Code 42C3), France Travail (Fiche Métier G1204), and INJEP sector surveys:
- **Hourly Billing Rate:**
  - Provincial metropolitan areas (Lyon, Marseille, Bordeaux, Lille, Nantes): 40 € to 60 € / hour.
  - Paris and Île-de-France: 60 € to 100 € / hour.
- **Client Roster Size:**
  - A full-time self-employed personal trainer maintains an active roster of 15 to 25 weekly private clients, conducting 20 to 35 billable sessions per week.
- **Client Churn & Lifecycle:**
  - Average quarterly client turnover: 30% to 45%.
  - Average client retention duration: 4 to 7 months for 1-on-1 private coaching.
  - Primary driver of churn: Loss of perceived progress velocity and administrative friction (inability of independent coaches to systematically track movement kinematics, velocity loss, or volume progression without manual paper logs).

## 2. The European Fitness Market

According to the *European Health & Fitness Market Report* published jointly by EuropeActive and Deloitte:
- **Total Market Revenues:** 31.8 billion € (growth of +14.0% year-on-year; estimated at 39.1 billion € when broader wellness and ancillary services are included).
- **Total Fitness Club Members:** 75.5 million registered members (up from 71.4 million, +5.7% annual growth).
  - Penetration rate: 8.3% of the European total population (Northern Europe > 20%, UK/Germany ~12–14%, France ~9.2%).
  - Strategic target: EuropeActive declared objective of 100 million members in European fitness facilities by 2030.
- **Fitness Facilities Count:** 67,515 commercial fitness clubs across Europe (+2.1% net annual growth).
- **Top Commercial Chain Operators by Members:**
  1. Basic-Fit (> 3.8 million members in 2023; > 4.2 million in 2024).
  2. PureGym (> 1.9 million members).
  3. RSG Group (> 1.5 million members across McFit, Gold's Gym, and John Reed).

## 3. Basic-Fit: French Footprint and Personal Training Model

According to Basic-Fit N.V.'s Annual Report and audited financial disclosures:
- **Footprint in France:**
  - 816 operating clubs in France as of year-end 2023 (expanded to over 860 clubs during 2024), representing more than 58% of the company's total network of 1,402 European clubs.
  - France member base: Approximately 2.45 million to 2.6 million active members.
- **Unit Metrics:**
  - Average Revenue Per User (ARPU): 23.53 € / month per member (up from 22.86 €).
  - Premium Tier Adoption: Over 44% of members subscribe to the Premium tier.
- **Personal Training Operational Model:**
  - *Zero Internal Employment:* Basic-Fit employs no salaried personal trainers in France.
  - *Concession / Rent Model:* Personal trainers operate as external independent contractors (micro-entreprises).
  - *Floor Rent (Access Fee):* Independent personal trainers pay a fixed monthly rental fee directly to Basic-Fit to operate commercially on club premises.
  - *Fee Structure:* Monthly rental fees range from 500 € to 1,500 € per trainer per club (depending on club tier and geographic density).
  - *Financial Reporting:* Rental income received from independent trainers is accounted for under "Other club revenue" (38.6 million € group revenue in 2023, up +22%).
  - *Trainer Economics:* The independent coach collects 100% of the coaching fee directly from the gym member (typically 45 € to 70 €/hour) and absorbs the monthly gym rental overhead.

## 4. The Equine Sector in France

Data compiled from the Institut Français du Cheval et de l'Équitation (IFCE / Observatoire Économique et Social du Cheval) and the Fédération Française d'Équitation (FFE):
- **Equine Athletes & Riders:**
  - Total Licensed Riders (Licences FFE): 648,328 licensees (3rd largest sports federation in France, behind football and tennis).
  - Gender Demographics: 83.4% of licensees are female (#1 female sport in France).
  - Total Population Practicing: 2.1 million to 2.5 million regular or occasional equestrians.
- **Equine Population:**
  - Approximately 1,020,000 horses, ponies, and donkeys in France.
  - 9,560 equestrian establishments (riding centers, pony clubs, boarding stables).
  - Direct employment: 17,800 FTEs in riding schools and livery stables; 66,000 active persons across the full equine economic sector.
- **Economic Weight:**
  - Direct economic flow: 6.2 billion € annually.
  - Equestrian sport and leisure direct turnover: 1.1 billion € generated by centers and stables.
- **Annual Expenditure per Horse Owner:**
  - Average annual cost per horse: 3,500 € to 7,200 € / year.
  - Stabling / Livery (Pension): 300 € to 650 € / month (up to 900 € / month in Île-de-France).
  - Farrier (Maréchalerie): 80 € to 120 € per trim/shoeing every 6–8 weeks (600 € to 900 € / year).
  - Veterinary Routine Care (Vaccines, worming, dental): 450 € to 850 € / year (diagnostic lameness workups average 1,500 € to 4,000 € when clinical issues occur).

- **License Count Evolution:**
  - 2022: 441,961
  - 2023: 445,306 (all-time peak)
  - 2024: 442,536
  - 2025: 446,547 (latest published census)
- **Growth Dynamics:**
  - Net 10-year cumulative growth: +9.6%.
  - Golf density: 63.8 licenses per 10,000 inhabitants.
- **Demographic & Socioeconomic Profile:**
  - Average Age: 54.2 years (Men: 53.1 years; Women: 57.4 years).
  - Gender Breakdown: 26.2% female, 73.8% male.
  - Member Retention: 83.2% annual licence renewal rate; > 91% multi-year retention rate.
  - Socio-Economic Index: Over 58% of adult players belong to CSP+ categories (executives, company owners, independent professions, higher education).

- **Infrastructure:**
  - 742 golf courses across metropolitan France (18-hole championship courses, 9-hole layouts, and driving range academies).

## 7. Comparative Technical Architecture Observations

1. **Hardware Friction Barrier:**
   Products requiring proprietary external sensors, docks, or wearable straps (Tempo Studio, Peloton Guide, early Hinge Health) encounter high procurement and distribution costs (hardware shipping, battery charging, sensor tethering). In contrast, solutions relying purely on standard monocular smartphone cameras (WorkoutVision, Sleip, Sportsbox AI, SwingVision, Kaia Health) eliminate hardware friction and inventory logistics.
2. **Compute Architecture & Cloud Dependency:**
   Many competitor platforms stream video frames to cloud GPU infrastructure for backend inference, incurring ongoing bandwidth and server compute costs (typically $0.05 to $0.20 per analysis minute on AWS/GCP GPU instances). WorkoutVision's 100% on-device WebAssembly and WebCodecs execution operates at zero server compute cost ($0.00 marginal infrastructure cost per user).
3. **Clinical Governance Gap:**
   While enterprise MSK platforms (Kaia Health, Hinge Health, Exer) publish peer-reviewed randomized controlled trials in indexed medical journals, consumer fitness apps frequently omit empirical validation and rely on unverified center-screen synthetic overlays.
