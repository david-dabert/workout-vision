# Failure anatomy of David's 33 real sets, and its critique (8 October 2026)

A read-only investigation (four agents: failure anatomy, digest of TRIED.md, state of the art, critic) of why the counting fails on
David's 13 real videos of 7 October and 20 stored sets. Scratch scripts and traces were kept outside the repository; the numbers below
come from the repository's own code (countReps, summarizeCount, pscCount, the learned model), which reproduced every value of
test/real-phone/learned-phase/lphase.txt. Status: findings, not rules; every remedy below must pass R2 before it ships.

## Failure modes (sets, reps lost, summed over core, PSC and the learned counter)

| Mode | Sets | Reps | What would fix it |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| 1. Extra movement at the start or end of the video (unrack and set-up, walk-out, getting on or off, racking, stillness) | 16 | 19 | Find the active (periodic) segment before counting and set the percentiles and thresholds on it. Measured: trimming bench side to 4.4-20.8 s takes the core from 5 to 8. Do not count a cycle that starts at the video's first sample below the rest threshold unless the head rule accepts it. Stop counting after a large posture change, such as the hips dropping 0.1-0.3 of the frame at a dismount. Learned counter: train with walking, racking, getting on and off, and still lead-ins as zero-rate negatives (its augmentation holds still for at most 3 s and has no walking). App: a trim handle, or guidance to start and stop the video at the lift. |
| 2. Learned rate miscalibrated inside the set (tempo, skeleton noise) | 7 | 14 | Retrain with time stretch covering 1.5-6 s per rep (its augmentation covers 0.6-1.5×) and with pose-noise augmentation (flips, jumps, dropouts). Calibrate the summed rate against a period estimate (PSC's lag). Use the learned count only to break ±1 ties. |
| 3. PSC picks the wrong period, the wrong signals or the wrong active segment (it does not know the exercise, and the bank is noisy) | 6 | 30 | When the exercise is known, make PSC aware of it. Weight the catalogue joint's signals; on the seated curl the elbow's P was only 0.12 at the wrong lag. Break period ties toward the core's median rep duration. Take the active segment from the core's first and last reps. Let the local period range beyond 0.7-1.4× the global lag, and use a local band where the amplitude drifts. Measure each change on the public halves (R2). |
| 4. Last rep cut by the end of the recording | 6 | 6 | App: ask the lifter to keep filming ~2 s after the last rep. Counter: test the shape match for a cut last rep (survey.ts SHAPE_EDGES part 'c', currently off), or let a rate estimate settle ±1 at the end. Measure under R2: the 1 October rule was withdrawn because movement after a set also reaches the working end. |
| 5. Catalogue joint misread at the working end: the machine or the plate occludes it, or the body folds, while visibility stays high (double peaks, phase inversion) | 6 | 8 | Check landmark plausibility before measuring the angle. For fixed-feet machine exercises, anchor the ankles. Scratch test: dropping samples whose ankle is more than 0.08-0.2 of the frame from its median takes the pendulum from 14 to 7 with aligned windows, but the same rule takes the barbell squats from 9 to 4-8, so it can only apply to machine keys. For the squat pattern, cross-check the knee against the hip angle: on its own, the hip angle (shoulder-hip-knee, rest high) counts both barbell squats 9, with every window on a true bottom and a range of 97-101° against the knee's 36-46°. Another option is vertical image displacement: PSC's mid-shoulder and mid-hip y are exact on all three squats. For back extensions, reject samples where the knee sits on the head's side of the hip. |
| 6. Joint hidden or pose lost: refusal or no count | 3 | 19 | The PSC proposal on refusals, shipped on 8 October, already gives 5011 the exact 5 to confirm; it gives nothing on bench angle. Filming guidance: side view, whole body in frame. A kneeling front view needs a signal that does not use the knee: trunk or shoulder height. |
| 7. PSC rest side inverted: it counts the gaps between reps (n-1) | 3 | 3 | When the exercise is known, orient PSC's consensus with the catalogue joint angle (its sign of correlation) and take the catalogue's rest end. Otherwise count the excursions into the working zone, edge fragments included. |
| 8. Partial read: the original video lacks frames | 3 | 15 | Nothing in the counter: the refusal is correct, and the label counts reps the file may not contain. If David still has the full video, a new export. |
| 9. The skeleton jumps to, or mixes with, a bystander | 2 | 5 | Keep the lifter: detect more than one pose per frame and keep the skeleton that continues the last one (PSC stage A is skipped). Treat a sample whose image centre moves more than ~8% of the frame in one step as lost and bridge it. On mse_18 the merged rep was 1.78× the median, just under SPLIT_LONG 1.8. |
| 10. Signal too small or at the threshold | 2 | 4 | Measure a rep's range to the rest level it reaches after the return, as placeBoundaries does, or lower the per-rep floor (push_up already uses 15°). Scratch: a 15° floor takes 5006 from 4 to 7 and 5008 from 3 to 6. MIN_ROM_DEGREES 20 is UNSOURCED. R2 on the scoreboard and the public halves first. |
| 11. PSC head rule rejects a first rep cut by the start of the video | 1 | 1 | Align PSC's head rule with the core's HEAD_RETURN_SHARE rule. |
| 12. Partial rep, or what the label counts | 1 | 1 | Ask David how he counts partial reps and a first press (R1: report, change no label). |
| 13. Left/right confusion | 0 | 0 | Enforce left/right continuity frame to frame (swap a pair back when the crossed match is closer than the straight one) before building signals. |
| 14. Tempo too fast | 0 | 0 | Nothing needed on these sets. |

## Critique (verbatim)

I reviewed the three investigations against the repository and ran read-only tests on a copy of HEAD. Nothing in the repository was edited, committed or pushed.

The main finding is that the proposed whole-body design has already been built and measured. It is `test/real-phone/template/sgc.js`, the spec-guided counter (SGC), committed on 8 October in 34e0365 and b694ec4. It is not recorded in TRIED.md, STATE.md, PLAN.md or BACKLOG.md, and none of the three investigations used its results. The measurements argue against it as a counter. What beats it is to keep the core as the only counter and use the rest of the body as a check that triggers a confirmation.

**Run conditions.** All runs used a `git archive` snapshot of de168e3. HEAD has since moved to 989a00c, where another agent committed a `fk.js` change (trunk pivot), so SGC numbers may shift slightly on today's HEAD. The 13 real videos are VP9 re-encodes read in desktop Chromium, not on the iPhone. squat_9_a reads 7 on a VP8 copy and 9 on VP9, so a one-set difference on those videos is within decode noise. ## 3. The proposed design: the evidence argues against it as a counter

- **Choosing and weighting several joints from the spec, with the path projection.** This is SGC.
  - It fixes the real-video misreads: pendulum 14→7, sissy 5006 4→7, chin-up 6→5.
  - It loses 15 exact sets overall. MM-Fit falls from 96 to 85 with 4 new sets off by 3; CF-Rep falls from 8 to 4.
  - It makes two of David's own sets catastrophic: stored back extension 13 goes from 12 to 3, and video back extension 18 from 17 to 13. That fails R2.
  - Its real-video gain comes from the body-position signals. With angles only (`SGC_ANGLES=1`), real videos fall to 3/8/3 (pendulum 11, squat 9_b 5).
- **Excluding frames that do not fit the exercise (scratch test).** I dropped catalogue-joint samples that sat more than half a band away from the body consensus (`sgcx/gate.txt`).
  - Real videos went from 6 to 3 exact: squat 9_a 7, 9_b 6, hanging leg raise 4, back extension 8 went to 3. The pendulum stayed at 14 with 40% of samples gated.
  - MM-Fit went from 96 to 90, Countix from 124 to 116.
  - As an edge discriminator it does not separate cases (`sgcx/edges.txt`). Agreement in the tail is 0.92 to 0.99 on the three real cut reps, but also 0.99 on set01 hip thrust 6's post-set tail, 0.82 on a press racking and 0.70 on another. Post-set moves lie on the exercise's path.
- **Angles measured in the plane of motion.** Nobody has tested this with correct pixel coordinates, and there are three blockers besides the aspect-ratio bug:
  - **Unsigned angles fold.** PSC's trunk signal is an unsigned angle from vertical. On back extension 8 the signed trunk spans −49° to +43°, with 38% of samples on the far side of vertical, so the unsigned trunk is not monotonic within a rep.
  - **Camera roll and facing flips.** On back extension 18 the image trunk sits 38° to 105° from the image's vertical (phone on its side). On back extension 13 the signed trunk spans −145° to +69° (facing flips). Signed angles need a stable forward direction and a known up, and world landmarks give neither.
  - **It would not fix the squats.** The probe's image knee range on the barbell squats is 40-56°, against 46-72° in world: the landmark is misplaced, not foreshortened.
- **Spec mismatch.** The back-extension spec starts upright and leans back (trunk 5 → −30, pitch 0 → −20). David's sets fold forward and cross vertical. On back extension 13 the hip correlates with the trunk at 0.32 (left) and −0.08 (right); on back extension 8, at −0.16.
- **Switching to the consensus when the joint disagrees** (`sgcx/rsc.txt`):
  - Real videos go from 6 to 7 exact (pendulum), but the chin-up becomes newly off by 3 (1 for 5).
  - Countix goes from 124 to 116 (2 gained, 10 lost).
  - Requiring every consensus signal to agree at 0.6 or more makes nothing switch on David's sets, and Countix still loses 4.

## 4. What would beat it

The single design I recommend is a body-checked core.
- **The core stays the only counter.** The catalogue joint and the core's rep logic keep producing the count.
- **The rest of the body becomes a check.** Alongside the count, compute SGC's signals combined by a weighted median, each weighted by how well it agrees with the others.
- **Low agreement triggers a confirmation, not a silent number.** When the joint and that consensus agree at less than 0.5, show the count to confirm, offer both candidate counts, and show no grade (R8). This is the same pattern as the PSC proposal on refusals.
- **It is R2-safe by construction.** It changes no count, so no exact count can fall.
- **It turns the largest real error into a question.** The pendulum's silent 14 becomes a choice between 14 and 7.
- **It protects the sets that are exact only by luck.** It flags all 4 compensation sets.
- **Cost:** about 17% of real-world sets get a confirmation prompt.

Honestly, no measured design that changes counts fixes the largest share without breaking clean sets.
- **By number of sets, edges are the largest share** (4 of the 9 deciding sets, all ±1). That needs David's cut-rep convention plus filming guidance, not a new counter.
- **The cheapest count-changing fix with an understood mechanism** is a per-rep floor consistent with the set's range gate. `push_up` already uses 15° (`core.ts` line 212). The anatomy's scratch run gives 5006 4→7 and 5008 3→6. It must go through `npm run scoreboard` and the public halves first (R2).

Image-plane angles need three changes before the next attempt is worth measuring:
- scale x by the frame's width over its height;
- use signed angles with the facing direction taken from the head landmarks;
- get gravity from the phone's motion sensor where the app records the video.

## Lessons from everything tried (digest of TRIED.md)

- Root cause shared by the edge ideas: one joint angle does not carry the context. The end rule (1 Oct), the edge warning, the edge re-read (5 Oct), stillStart (6 Oct), T4 head/cut (7 Oct), HEAD_RETURN_SHARE 0.45, the lunge first-rep and push-up head-rep variants all failed because a cut rep, a shallow first rep or a held working posture has the same angle trace as setup or an after-set move. Every reopen condition in TRIED.md names the same missing input: a second discriminator such as the set's rhythm or the pose before or after the hold. None has been built.
- Root cause shared by the signal-stage ideas: the error sits in the pose input, not in the conditioning. Outlier windows, the bridge, T2 interpolation, T3 z-weight, ending a rep at a null (FINDING-010) and choosing the larger-range side (FINDING-011) each lost sets. The analysts found 255 of 277 inexact half-A sets unaffected by 13 signal-stage variants (TRIED.md line 26). synth.txt shows the pose model's world landmarks lose about 30 % of range before any smoothing.
- Root cause shared by the side-choice ideas: on real video the side that moves more is often the noisier one, and in side views MediaPipe reports a hidden limb as visible (symmetry/README.md, SI -87 %). Per-sample side switching (30 Sep), the larger-range side, the lunge fallbacks on the smoother knee or the larger span, and joining pull-up elbows when neither is countable all failed. What worked: count each side whole and keep the side with more reps when it is seen on at least half the samples (eitherSide), or join two sides only when their angles correlate, pairing a lagging rep (together).
- Root cause shared by the pose-stage ideas: none of them checks that a pose belongs to the lifter doing this exercise. The backward pass filled noisy single samples. The lifter lock rested on one frame's box size. The jump gate rested on one frame's torso, and the lifter's own skeleton jumps more than a torso length 13-152 times per set. The collapsed-torso gate ran on One-Euro-filtered landmarks. All passed the clean synthetic bodies and failed on David's 5 to 13 real videos. Only the crop retry, seeded from the last accepted pose, shipped.
- Root cause shared by the class-agnostic counters: without the exercise they pick the most periodic motion, which is not always the rep. The period counter, PSC, the learned counters, the HMM, whole-body PCA and motion rhythm all win on clips cut to their reps (Countix, MM-Fit, synthetic) and lose on whole recordings with setup and rest (RepCount-A, David's videos), which is the kind the app records. No selector found which counter is right: the learned choice alone lost (A 164 -> 159), agreement was not a gate, and PSC disagreement flags 62 % of the core's exact Countix counts. ## State of the art (insights; sources in the agents' reports, several primary sources unreachable from this environment)

- Evidence limits. arXiv, CVF open access, PMC, MDPI, Google Patents and USPTO could not be fetched from this session: WebFetch failed with 'getaddrinfo ENOTFOUND arxiv.org' (and for openaccess.thecvf.com, pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov, patents.google.com), and curl got 'CONNECT tunnel failed, response 403'. Three levels of evidence follow. Code read directly: PoseRAC, joint-angle PoseRAC, the ML Kit classifier and counter, RepNet-PyTorch. READMEs and model cards: TransRAC, SSTRAC, ESCounts, TempoRAC, PushUpBench, RepNet weights. Abstracts from the Hugging Face papers API: RepNet, TransRAC, ESCounts, ExRAC, Riccio, PushUpBench. Every other number comes from search-engine extracts of the paper pages and is marked unverified wherever its table was not seen. Nothing in the repository was written; scratch files are under scratchpad/understand/. git status shows test/real-phone/synth/motions/README.md modified and test/real-phone/template/fk.test.ts untracked; I made no edits to either.
- The headline pose-based results are not achievable numbers. PoseRAC's eval.py (lines 81-129) counts each test video under all 8 class heads and keeps the class whose count is closest to gt_count (line 56). Its README result, MAE 0.2356 / OBO 0.5592, is therefore a best-class-in-hindsight oracle. The joint-angle follow-up (eval.py lines 84-134) does the same for its 0.2109 / 0.5921. Count What Repeats (CVPR 2026 Findings) claims RepCount OBO 0.842; an independent MediaPipe reproduction on a UCFRep dev split got OBO 0.298 / NMAE 0.643 and missed its own gate. TransRAC's MAE uses ground-truth density maps: under the standard protocol it is 0.6099 / OBO 0.2763, not 0.4431 / 0.2913. 'RepNet' on RepCount-A appears as OBO 0.0134 in later tables, but 0.5329 by its own authors with multi-speed inference. The honest pose-only, class-agnostic reference is SSTRAC: MAE 0.373 / OBO 0.312 on about 500 single-person RepCount-A clips. Against that, PSC's within-one on the RepCount-A build half, 56 of 124 = 45% (TRIED.md line 74), is not far below the reproducible state of the art.
- The counting logic of the best documented pose counters is the same as the core's. PoseRAC uses an EMA (momentum 0.4) with hysteresis at 0.78 / 0.4 (RepCount_pose_config.yaml). ML Kit uses an EMA (window 10, alpha 0.2) with enter 6 / exit 4 of 10 nearest neighbours (RepetitionCounter.java, EMASmoothing.java). What differs is the signal fed into that logic. They use a whole-body 'rest pose vs working pose' score computed from all 33 landmarks: a learned per-frame classifier in PoseRAC, whose model.py line 35 sets sequence length 1, so it has no temporal model; k-NN on torso-normalised landmark differences in ML Kit. The core uses one catalogue joint angle. Neither approach needs per-user data, but both need two pose classes per exercise. The repo's motion specs already define those poses (u=0 and u=1) and render them through the app's pose model. A per-exercise two-pose score is the most direct transfer, and it is untested. SGC (test/real-phone/template/sgc.js) uses the spec's expected signal directions, not a pose classifier.
- The main thing the best systems have and a threshold counter lacks is a per-frame estimate of whether this moment is part of the repeating motion. RepNet has a periodicity head, OVRCounter localises the repeats, Rethinking-TSM predicts per-frame start probabilities, and Apple counts in sliding 90-frame windows. Without it, setup, racking and walking are counted or break the count: Metric's bench 'ghost reps' from racking (PeerJ 2024), and RepNet failing on untrimmed OVR clips (MAE 0.86). The repo's own measurements show the same gap. PSC's duration/period wins on clips cut to their reps (Countix 162, MM-Fit 136 exact) but loses on whole videos (RepCount 14, real video 3) (TRIED.md line 75). The learned counter is 'best on clips cut to their reps and worst on whole recordings' (line 83). TReViS (Sept 2026) suggests a concrete next step for the learned-phase augmentation: insert non-repeating filler around and between synthetic repetitions, so the model learns where the set is.
- One reference rep is worth a lot, and the app can get one without asking the user. FCA-RAC, given the first cycle at test time, reaches RepCount-A OBO 0.47 against TransRAC's 0.29. ESCounts' exemplars take RepCount OBO from 0.39 to 0.56. ExRAC/CaRaCount (wearables) and Sarsfield's single-exemplar subsequence DTW (skeleton) count by matching an exemplar. The repo tried DTW only on one joint angle, to accept edge or split candidates (survey T4, TRIED.md line 55; failed on half B, 235 to 233). Untested: an exemplar counter over PSC's multi-joint signal bank, with the exemplar taken from the catalogue spec rendered through the pose model, or from the set's most confident rep. It could be a counter or a second opinion on PSC's proposals. It needs no per-user data. The cost is O(N x M) DTW at 15 Hz, which is cheap in a browser.
- Multi-speed inference is standard in learned and periodicity counters. RepNet-PyTorch's run.py tries temporal strides 1, 2, 3, 4 and 8 and keeps the most self-consistent. Apple's Downsampler factor 3 handles about 1 rep per 3 s. Count What Repeats aggregates peak detection over scales. The core has fixed time constants (SG window 0.333 s, MIN_REP_SEC 0.5, MAX_REP_SEC 8; core.ts lines 293-300), and PSC searches lags of 0.8-8 s with a local period. A threshold counter is mostly speed-independent, so this matters for any fixed-window learned model (Apple's 90 frames; the learned-phase TCN's 17 s receptive field) and should be added there as stride augmentation or test-time multi-stride.
- Synthetic data is the standard trick, but its gap to real video is measured and large. RepNet, TReViS, PAAE (skeleton synthesis by interpolation and diffusion) and Li et al. 2023 (synthetic skeleton augmentation) all train on constructed repetitions whose counts are known by construction. FLAG3D shows the rendered-to-real cost for fitness recognition from 2D pose: 98.6% in-domain against 81.6% when trained on rendered and tested on real (2s-AGCN). The repo's own ablation agrees: rendered sets helped David's real videos (mean error 1.38 against 1.69) but changed nothing on RepCount-A (4.32 against 4.27) (TRIED.md line 87). Licensing matters for a product. AMASS is non-commercial; HumanML3D and text-to-motion models trained on it (MoMask code is MIT) inherit that. The repo's hand-posed specs on three.js Mixamo avatars avoid the AMASS dependency.
- Breadth costs accuracy, and no published method covers hundreds of exercises without per-exercise data. Narrow per-exercise rule systems report very high agreement: the MDL state machines 97.2% on 12 exercises (1,513 videos), Pūioio 98.89% on 3, Metric over 99% squat recall. The only system at the app's breadth, LiFT (1,900+ exercises, about 7.6k labelled videos), reports 85.3% within one and 76.5% recognition, with no code released. Apple's class-agnostic HumanBodyActionCounter publishes no accuracy. So the catalogue tiers (a counter only where an exercise is shown to count), with spec-derived per-exercise signals, match what the evidence supports. A class-agnostic counter (PSC) remains a fallback to confirm, as shipped on 8 October (TRIED.md line 88).
- What can run in an iPhone browser alongside MediaPipe. Pose-input models only. PoseRAC's checkpoint is 10,772,679 bytes in fp32, about 2.69M parameters by my estimate, about 2.7 MFLOP per frame. The repo's own learned TCN is 65,608 bytes and runs at 115 ms per minute of video in Node (TRIED.md line 82). ML Kit k-NN, DTW templates and spectrogram counters (Hsu) cost almost nothing. RGB models are out: RepNet's checkpoint is 102,830,765 bytes (ResNet50V2, 64 frames at 112 px, several strides), TransRAC uses Video Swin-T, ESCounts a VideoMAE-v2 ViT-B, CountLLM an LLM. Apple's counter is on-device but reachable only from Swift (Create ML Components, iOS 16+). VLMs are both off-device and weak: on PushUpBench the best frontier model gets 42.1% exact and Qwen3-VL-4B 11.89%.
- Pose-failure handling in published systems has close analogues already in the repo. Pūioio pauses state transitions once pose confidence stays low (threshold starting at 1.5 s) and uses optical flow meanwhile. SSTRAC reconstructs defective skeletons before counting. D2-STX fuses pose with RGB at joint and pixel level. The repo has motionRhythm.js, the backward pass, the crop retry, the lifter lock and the jump gate (TRIED.md lines 68-98). No published browser-ready method goes further than these.
- Datasets and comparisons the app could add. PushUpBench is CC-BY-4.0: 446 clips (227 test), human counts given as lists of acceptable values. It would be a public exam through the app's pose path, if David accepts it under R1 and a rule for multi-value counts is fixed. Hsu et al.'s training-free whole-skeleton self-similarity spectrogram reports MM-Fit OBO 0.94 (normalised MAE 0.06). That is roughly comparable to the core's MM-Fit within-one of 146/158 = 92.4% and PSC's 136/158 = 86.1% (TRIED.md line 74), but the protocols differ. Its 'integrate the spectrogram' readout, a count with no peak picking, has not been tried in PSC. Countix-Fitness-pose (GMFL-Net, 553 videos), Rep-Fitness (PAAE) and Fit3D (611 sequences with per-rep bounds) would need their licences checked.
- Intellectual property, to flag to David (not legal advice; claim text not read because patent sites were unreachable). Google LLC holds US 12,469,290 B2, granted 11 Nov 2025, with 23 claims: class-agnostic repetition counting through a temporal self-similarity matrix bottleneck, i.e. the RepNet method. PSC stage C is a self-similarity lag profile over pose signals. NEX Team (HomeCourt) holds US 11,450,010: smartphone pose landmarks, a principal-component projection, and repetitions found from the pattern. Levy and Wolf hold the US 10,460,194 family on counting with a trained network. Freedom to operate for PSC and any RepNet-like learned head has not been checked.
- Ranked transfers for this app, each a counting change that must pass npm run scoreboard (R2). (1) A per-frame 'inside the set' signal for whole recordings: a periodicity head or filler-augmented training on the learned-phase net (RepNet, OVR, TReViS). (2) Per-exercise two-pose classifiers on all landmarks, from motion-spec renders, fed into the existing hysteresis (PoseRAC and ML Kit without the oracle). (3) A multi-joint exemplar or template counter, as a second opinion on PSC proposals (FCA-RAC, ESCounts, Sarsfield). (4) Multi-stride test-time inference for any windowed learned model (RepNet, Apple). (5) Self-supervised pretraining of the pose encoder on unlabelled landmark sequences (SimPer, PAMS-TCC), since the repo holds many unlabelled sets. Not worth pursuing on a phone: RGB backbones, VLMs, and chasing published RepCount-pose numbers, which are oracle or unreproduced.
