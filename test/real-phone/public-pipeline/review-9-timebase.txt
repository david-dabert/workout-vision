**Defect 1: a set already at 15 per second does not come out unchanged. Its unseen mask roughly doubles, so training no longer matches what the app computes.**
- **Where:** data.py lines 64-65 and 69:
  ```
  j = np.clip(np.searchsorted(ts, grid, side='right') - 1, 0, len(ts) - 1)
  k = np.clip(j + 1, 0, len(ts) - 1)
  ...
  ok = (seen[j] > 0) & (seen[k] > 0)
  ```
- **Why:** when a grid point falls on an original sample (or just before it, from float rounding), there is no gap to interpolate across. Still, `k` is always the next sample and must also be seen. So a sample that was seen is marked unseen whenever the sample after it was missed.
- **Scenario:** a clip at exactly 15 per second, as `load()` passes it (float32 timestamps, line 81), with pose missed on samples 20, 50 and 80:
  - the result has unseen samples 19, 20, 49, 50, 51, 80 and 81;
  - with float64 timestamps it has 19, 20, 49, 50, 79 and 80;
  - the positions are unchanged (largest difference 2.4e-5).
- **App side:** `learnedFeatures` in /home/user/workout-vision/src/lib/counting/learned.ts sets the mask to 0 only on the missed frame itself. So one missed frame is one unseen sample in the app, but two or three in training.
- **Real data:** on the admitted public build sets, the unseen share goes from 12.48% (62,991 samples) to 15.87% (63,144 samples). That is about 27% more unseen samples, which the network learns from on the mask channel (channel 37).
- **At 8 per second** (gaps of 0.125 s), one missed frame becomes 3 or 4 unseen samples. That follows the "both neighbours" rule, but the grid points that fall on a sample are the part that is wrong.
- **Why the test misses it:** the "a set already at 15 comes out unchanged" check (test_objective.py lines 64-66) only asserts the length, with an all-seen mask and float64 timestamps.
- **Smallest test that fails today:**
  ```
  t = np.arange(0, 2.0001, 1/15); m = np.ones(len(t), np.float32); m[10] = 0
  _, s, _ = data.on_timebase(np.zeros((len(t), 12, 3), np.float32), m, t)
  assert (s == m).all()   # fails: s is 0 at indices 9 and 10
  ```
  Adding a float32 version (`t.astype(np.float32)`) covers what `load()` actually passes.
