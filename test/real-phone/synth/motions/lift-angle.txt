The prone Y raise read as the arms' lift against gravity (core.ts, LiftDefinition.lift), on rendered sets (8 October 2026).
Bench: test/real-phone/synth/motions/lift-angle.test.ts. Renders: library.mjs prone_y_raise, through run.mjs (the app's pose model);
the landscape option is synth.js P.landscape (it keeps the 52-degree field of view on the short side, so the long side
widens to 82 degrees: orientation and lens change together). Bodies: Michelle (plain clothing) and Soldier (dark,
patterned clothing). A rendered body is not a person; no person has been filmed doing this exercise for the app.
Counted on the better seen arm (the first form kept the arm with more reps, eitherSide, which a far arm's rest can
fool: review of 8 October, src/lib/counting/__tests__/lift.test.ts). Not offered in the app (offer.js, NOT_YET_COUNTED).
Status: experimental.

Columns: per side, the range of each signal (10th to 90th percentile, degrees) and, in brackets, its correlation with the
rendered progress through the reps; then the reps rendered and the app's result (summarizeCount, withProposal,
withBodyCheck; 'to confirm': the result screen would ask for the count, R8). The renders keep no image landmarks, so
the body check here reads world signals only; the app's adds image-plane ones. For this exercise its consensus is the
arms' own wrist and elbow heights (spec-profiles.json), so it cannot see a misread arm: not a safeguard here.
